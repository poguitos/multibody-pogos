# Phase 7: the vehicle layer in ISO 8855 axes

- **Dates:** 14 and 15 November 2026
- **Plan:** task 7.1; finding F11; D3, D60
- **Commits:** `d9f9466` (the scenarios recorded before the move), then see
  git log
- **Written:** at the time

## Goal

Task 7.1: "Axes (F11). Move the vehicle layer to ISO 8855. Remove every
hard-coded 'up'. *Done when the same manoeuvre gives the same physical result
before and after.*"

## Starting point

The vehicle layer (the tyres, aerodynamics, anti-roll bar, the two vehicle
builders, the suspension corners, equilibrium, toe and camber, the lap
vehicle) used X forward, Y up, and called +Z "left". Finding F11 of the
October review had read it: with Y up, +Z is geometrically the right, so the
code described the mirror image of the car its labels named, consistently.
World Y as up was written into the tyre's contact, the aerodynamics' ride
height and horizontal speed, the anti-roll bar, the builders' gravity and
the equilibrium. The core assumed nothing; the HMMWV benchmark of task 5.4,
a kernel model, was already Z-up. D3 had fixed the target signs in
`docs/conventions.md`.

## What was done

1. **Reading before changing.** The tyre's lateral axis was up x forward,
   which with Y up is -Z: the physical left, the label "right". A positive
   steer turned the wheel toward +Z, the label "left". So in its own labels
   the old code ran a mirror-image world in which "left" was +Z, and the
   tyre's law, odd in the slip angle with no shift terms, is symmetric under
   that mirror. Hence the plan of D60: keep every label and swap Y and Z in
   all data; every result read through the labels should be unchanged.
2. **Scenarios for the comparison, recorded first** (`d9f9466`): the
   regression scenarios on the template car used simple corners or one
   wishbone, so two were added with references from the legacy code. The
   double-wishbone sports car with anti-roll bars and downforce whose centre
   of pressure is off the centre line, turning left; the front-wheel-drive
   McPherson hatchback braking in a right turn. Each records the chassis and,
   for each corner, its tyre's vertical and lateral forces, toe and camber.
   Their sensitivity was measured (a 1e-12 change of gravity: 5.0e-4 and
   2.3e-4 of the tolerance; 1e-6: 500 and 234 times it) and CI ran them on
   Linux within 1.8e-5 and 9.0e-5 of the tolerance.
3. **The move.** `mbd/vehicle/axes.hpp` (`iso_gravity`, `ground_up`); the
   tyres and the aerodynamics measure along the ground's normal, a setting
   the builders give from gravity; the anti-roll bar along the chassis's Z;
   toe and camber toward +Y; the bump drivers along world Z; the builders'
   gravity, corner positions, simple corners' sliders (Rx(pi): the joint's Z
   to -Z), the steering rack (Rx(-pi/2): its Z to the chassis's Y) and
   equilibrium height (the free joint's third coordinate); the lap vehicle's
   height. The data's numeric triples were swapped by a script that printed
   each of its 46 changes.
4. **The comparison.** The fast regression scenarios against the legacy
   references: the kernel scenarios, `lap-oval`, `sedan-modes` and
   `wishbone-sweep` passed unchanged (their outputs keep their meaning under
   the change); the four with axes failed, as they should. Their references
   were written again, and a script compared each new reference with its
   legacy one through the mapping (below).
5. **The tests**, sixteen files: 80 replacements prepared while a build ran
   and applied by a script that checks every one matches exactly; the build
   then ran the suite.
6. **Documentation:** `docs/conventions.md`'s axes section, now a statement
   of what is, with the legacy frame as one paragraph of history;
   troubleshooting; `docs/python.md`'s note on what is not bound.

## Decisions

D60: keep the labels (the mirror), up from gravity through one header, the
proof by regression scenarios. Smaller ones: `VehicleModel::chassis_ty_idx`
renamed `chassis_tz_idx`, `KinematicSweepPoint::wheel_y` renamed
`wheel_height`; `extract_camber` keeps its sign toward +Y, now said plainly
in the conventions (for a right wheel, ISO camber is its negative).

## What went wrong, and how it was found

- **The arms' inertia box left unswapped.** The swapping script was told to
  keep the arms' box (0.02, 0.02, 0.2), on the belief that it lay in a frame
  along the arm's hinge. The comparison showed the sports car's pitch rate
  off by 3.8e4 times the tolerance (1.7e-5 rad/s), the hatchback's by
  1.5e4, the simple-cornered sedan at rounding: the difference was in the
  linkage corners. The arms' joint frames turn into the hinge and back, so
  the arm's body frame is the chassis's at rest, and the box was along the
  legacy lateral axis. Swapped; all four then agreed to rounding. Without
  the comparison, every test would have passed: the box changes the arms'
  inertia, not any asserted value.
- **The sedan's description.** The regression scenario `sedan-drive` was
  called "the double-wishbone sedan", in its description and the README;
  `DefaultSedan` has simple corners. Corrected with its reference.
- **Escapes in scripted edits, twice:** a `\n` in a replacement string that
  no longer matched the file, and `\b` written as backspace characters into
  the test-edit script; both seen before anything was written (the first by
  the script's own check, the second by reading the line's bytes). And
  `sed -i` turned two CRLF files to LF; restored.

## Verification

- **The comparison, through the mapping** (positions and velocities: Y and
  Z swapped; angular velocities: swapped and negated; tyres' lateral forces:
  negated; the rest unchanged), the worst channel of each scenario against
  its tolerance, 1e-8 of the channel's largest magnitude:

  | Scenario | Mapped | Unmapped |
  |---|---|---|
  | sedan-drive | 3.2e-6 | 9.9e8 |
  | sports-car-turn | 1.2e-4 | 2.7e8 |
  | hatchback-brake | 1.3e-5 | 1.9e10 |
  | sedan-slalom (20 s) | 2.6e-6 | 2.0e9 |

  `sedan-modes` (frequencies and damping) and `wishbone-sweep` (camber, toe,
  height) passed against their legacy references without being written
  again. That is the "done when": the same manoeuvres give the same
  physical results, to rounding.
- **The tests** keep their derivations; each axis-dependent line moved with
  the axes. One check was made stronger: a tyre sliding across its heading
  must push against the sliding (-X), with nothing along its heading and
  k delta vertically, where it only required some horizontal force. The
  anti-roll bar's chassis moment is now +720 N m (the mirror of the legacy
  -720): rolling the right side down, ISO's positive roll.
- **The suite:** 546 tests pass (544 before the task; the two new
  scenarios), the build without a warning, 121.48 s with the laptop on
  battery.

## Lessons

- **Record the "before" first.** The two scenarios written by the old code
  turned "the same physical result" into a number, and found the one data
  error no test could.
- **Check where a frame is before deciding what is in it.** The arms' frame
  was assumed, not read.
- **Keep the labels when a frame was mirrored.** The mirror changed nothing
  any user or test reads.
