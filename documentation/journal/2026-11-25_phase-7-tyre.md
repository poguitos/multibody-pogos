# Phase 7: the tyre

- **Dates:** 25 November 2026

## Goal

Task 7.4: "Tyre (F7). Magic Formula 5.2 with its full coefficient set, a
`.tir` file reader, camber, aligning and overturning moments, rolling
resistance, combined slip, and scaling factors. Lag through relaxation
length, valid to standstill. *Done when curves match the reference curves in
Pacejka chapter 4 and an independent Magic Formula implementation, and a car
held on its brakes on a slope stays put.*"

## Starting point

The tyre on a wheel (`FullTireForce`, task 7.3's contact) took the simple
formula of `mbd/forces/pacejka.hpp`, older than the plan: a few coefficients
per axis, a cornering stiffness constant in load (F7 measured 45 kN/rad from
2 to 8 kN), no moments, no camber, no rolling resistance, no lag, and slips
over max(|Vx|, 1 m/s), so that at rest a tyre was a damper and a car on its
brakes on a slope crept (F7). The kernel had state elements since task 4.1,
made with a tyre's lag in mind. The book (Pacejka, 3rd ed., 2012) gives MF
6.1 in section 4.3.2 and the transient models in chapters 7 and 8; no MF 5.2
manual was within reach. The author chose both versions, the file choosing,
and MFeval as the independent implementation.

## What was done

**The steady-state tyre (first part, commit 0e5515a).**

1. The book's section 4.3.2 was read from its PDF (decoded from pdftotext,
   whose font maps symbols to other characters). Project Chrono's PAC2002
   tyre (BSD-3) was read for MF 5.2's camber scaling; it mixes 5.2 and 6.1
   forms, so it served as a reading, not a source.
2. A first version followed the book line by line, both versions in one
   evaluation, every parameter once in an X-macro (section, name, default);
   the .tir reader (`mbd/io/tir_file.hpp`) with its messages, MBD-I020 to
   I024 and MBD-F053; the book's Appendix 3 tyre written out as a .tir file
   and a synthetic MF 5.2 tyre; tests of identities the equations imply
   (slip stiffnesses are the slopes, peaks are mu Fz plus the shifts,
   combined slip leaves a force alone where the other slip is zero, the
   rolling resistance and relaxation lengths at the nominal state).
3. The author downloaded MFeval (a File Exchange sign-in). Its documentation
   lists seven places where the book's forms did not match TNO's solver and
   the paper of Besselink, Schmeitz and Pacejka (2010) did; the paper's open
   manuscript, fetched from TU Eindhoven, has TNO's MF-Tyre 6.1 in an
   appendix. D63: the paper's forms where it and the book differ. The
   evaluation was rewritten to them: the slip angle and inclination as they
   are, the trail's camber terms, cos(alpha) once in the residual torque,
   the equivalent slips, the rolling resistance (-R0 Fz0, against the
   rolling), qsx12 to qsx14 modelled instead of refused, no degressive
   friction factor; MF 5.2 as the same with 5.2's forms where the paper
   says 6.1 changed them.
4. A MATLAB script, kept with MFeval outside the repository, writes MFeval's
   curves for three tyres (Appendix 3; Appendix 3 with every scaling factor
   other than 1; the MF 5.2 tyre with scaling factors) to CSV: 1812 points,
   thirteen channels. MFeval fills a coefficient a file leaves out with a
   sample tyre's value (QSX4 = 5); the script gives the engine's defaults
   instead and lists which. The comparison passed at the first build. Each
   channel matched to the CSV's rounding (3.5e-12) but the trail and Mz,
   2.1e-7 and 1.1e-7, both at the slowest point: MFeval's cos(alpha') =
   Vcx / (Vc + 1e-6). The tolerances were set to two tiers from that.
5. Each of the book's forms was put back alone, rebuilt and compared: every
   one failed, by 0.09 % to 9 % of a channel (the rolling resistance 75 %).
   The table is in `docs/tyres.md`.

**The tyre on a wheel (second part).**

6. `FullTireForce` was rewritten on the Magic Formula (D64): a parameter set
   gives the radius, the vertical stiffness and damping and VXLOW as well;
   two states, the tread's deflections, by the book's semi-non-linear model
   (8.110 to 8.114) with its low-speed damping (8.127, 8.128); the
   inclination from the wheel's axis and the road's normal; MBD-F054 for a
   set without relaxation lengths. The tyre is a state element, and the
   simulator now attaches a force element that is one when it is made.
7. The vehicles' parameters hold a `tyre` (the Appendix 3 tyre by default,
   `pacejka_appendix3_tyre()`) in place of the radius, stiffnesses and the
   simple tyre's coefficients. The bicycle model reduces a vehicle's tyre
   to the simple formula (`PacejkaTireParams::FromMagicFormula`).
8. While rewriting the vehicle tests, the symmetric bounce test showed what
   the Magic Formula's ply steer and conicity would do to a car whose four
   tyres push the same way: TYRESIDE is read, and a tyre on the other side
   is mirrored, as TNO's solver does.
9. The suite then failed 22 tests, and the regression's vehicle scenarios.
   The causes, found one at a time, are below; they gave TNO's low-speed
   fade of the shifts in the Magic Formula, the rolling resistance's ramp
   over 0.01 m/s, and new expectations in the tests that relied on the old
   tyre at rest.
10. The six vehicle scenarios of the regression harness were written again,
    and the sensitivity probe run over every scenario.

## Decisions

- D63: the Magic Formula's equations are TNO's MF-Tyre 6.1 as Besselink,
  Schmeitz and Pacejka (2010) publish them, where the book differs; MF 5.2
  is the same with 5.2's forms where the paper says 6.1 changed them; the
  chapter 4 curves read as the chapter's equations with the book's one
  published tyre; MFeval as the independent implementation.
- D64: the tyre on a wheel: the parameter set its one source; the slips
  lagging by the semi-non-linear model; mirrored by side; the shifts faded
  at rest; the rolling resistance ramped over 0.01 m/s; attached by the
  simulator; every vehicle's tyre.
- Tests that compare with hand calculations set aside what they do not test
  and say so: the rolling resistance in the forward-force and half-pedal
  braking tests; the shifts and the overturning couple for a wheel balanced
  on its edge; the lag in the steering geometry test and in the implicit
  methods' tests at walking pace.

## What went wrong, and how it was found

- **The book's printed forms are not the industry's.** The first version
  followed (4.E1) to (4.E78) literally. Its identity tests passed, as they
  would for either form: none of them touches the terms that differ. Only
  MFeval's documentation, and then the paper, showed that TNO's solver uses
  other forms in seven places. The comparison would have caught it, but
  reading first saved a round of chasing differences.
- **At rest the Magic Formula pushes.** A fitted curve does not pass through
  zero at zero slip: the Appendix 3 tyre gives 19 N forward and about 70 N
  sideways at no slip and no deflection. The first sign was a wheel balanced
  on its edge that tipped over in a test (the shifts and the overturning
  couple). Then statics, linearisation, the allocation test (whose
  generalised-alpha step did not converge at t = 0 on the double-wishbone
  sedan) and the equilibrium tests failed: a car at rest had no static
  equilibrium. TNO's low-speed model, as MFeval has it, fades the shifts,
  the trail and the residual torque below VXLOW; with it, six of the 22
  failures went.
- **Four tyres from one file steer a car.** Without mirroring the ply steer
  and conicity of all four tyres point the same way. Seen while rewriting
  the symmetric bounce test's expectations (lateral, roll and yaw within
  1e-6), before running it; TNO mirrors a tyre mounted on the other side.
- **A car rolled away on level ground.** The double-wishbone sedan, left
  free after settling, rolled backwards at 4 cm/s within 5 s. Printing its
  tyres' forces each second showed side forces of 294 and 338 N on each
  tyre, left and right opposed (the treads deflected as the contacts moved
  across while the car settled), on wheels toed out 0.83 and 0.95 degrees:
  17.3 N along the road. The rolling resistance, ramped in over VXLOW (1
  m/s), barely resisted at walking pace. Ramped over 0.01 m/s, as dry
  friction smoothed, the car creeps at 1.8 mm/s.
- **Tests that relied on the old tyre's stiff damper at rest.** The
  implicit methods' tests at walking pace were built on a spin mode of about
  3000 per second, which RK4 could not step at 5 ms; with the lag it is gone
  (RK4 steps it within 1.3e-3). They now run on the tyre without lag. The
  stop tests found the rock-back: the sliding front tyres give back their
  treads' deflection when the car stops (up to three times the sliding slip
  over the relaxation length, the limit of (8.112)), the car rocks back past
  the rear tyres' balance, and a rear wheel slips back against its brake at
  up to 11.7 rad/s for a few milliseconds. The guard against wheels turning
  backwards (task 4.2's brake direction) now holds while the car moves.
- **The slope test's first hand calculation** took only the tyres' springs:
  1.07 mm against 3.50 measured. A locked car on its tyres pitches as a body
  about the contacts; the pitch on the springs adds h M / k_pitch = 2.31
  mm: 3.38 mm, and the ringing's relaxation of the treads the rest.
- **The slope test first ran on the level**: it tilted gravity before
  building the car, and the builder sets gravity. Every number it printed
  was zero, which gave it away.
- **Three of the new tests' expectations were wrong at first:** a sign (a
  deflection of -1e-4 m gives cx u, negative), the cosine of (8.128) at
  0.01 m/s (769.81, not 770 N s/m), and a tolerance that left out the side
  force curve's offset from zero slip (5e-4 of the slope there).
- **Tooling:** heredocs with unbalanced apostrophes failed in the shell
  twice (scripts were then written with the editor); the first run of the
  probes built nothing, the build script's path having lost its backslash
  in nested quoting (every probe reported "build failed"); a regular
  expression cut the Appendix 3 tyre's name from the margins (a capital R).

  the other slip, rolling resistance and relaxation lengths at the nominal

  parameter; a road's friction is LMUX and LMUY scaled; the reduction to the

- `test_magic_formula_mfeval`: three tyres, 1812 points, thirteen channels

## Lessons

- Before writing a model from a textbook, find what the software its data
  are fitted with does. The book's printed Magic Formula differs from TNO's
  solver in seven places, by up to 9 % of a channel (75 % for the rolling
  resistance), and every .tir file is fitted with TNO's tools.
- An independent implementation is worth most where its author compared it
  with the reference; where it is the only source it checks nothing (MF
  5.2's rolling resistance and relaxation lengths, the one observation
  about Gyk). Say so in the decision.
- A fitted curve's offsets are physics only while rolling. At rest they make
  a car with no equilibrium, a wheel that tips, a test that cannot settle.
- When a stiff damper is replaced by a lightly damped spring, every test
  that relied on the damper to stop motion shows it: "settled" thresholds,
  integrator demonstrations, the ends of drives. Each failure here was the
  model telling the truth about the new tyre, once understood; none was a
  reason to loosen a tolerance without a derivation.
- Write the hand calculation for the mechanism that dominates: on the slope
  the pitch was twice the tyre's spring.

## Open ends

- Rolling backwards is not compared with any reference (MFeval's
  conventions for it differ).
- The low-speed damping is the book's, light: a car rings on its tyres after
  a stop. A real tyre's hysteresis would damp more; `k_V_low0` is a
  parameter, without data to set it.
- The deflection limit lets a sliding tyre store three times the sliding
  slip over its relaxation length, and give it back at a stop (the
  rock-back). The book's fully non-linear model (8.116), whose relaxation
  length shortens with slip, would store less.
- No static rolling resistance: a free car with a push from its settling
  creeps at millimetres per second.
- Linearisation holds the state elements' states, so the tyres' lag is not
  in the modes (a kernel extension).
- Turn slip, the vertical tyre of the file (loaded radius, effective rolling
  radius), the contact patch, SWIFT.
- The tyre evaluates the Magic Formula twice per evaluation (the slips need
  the relaxation lengths first): to look at in the real-time phases.
- A vehicle has fast states again: multirate's vehicle test (task 4.6),
  removed by 7.3, could return.
