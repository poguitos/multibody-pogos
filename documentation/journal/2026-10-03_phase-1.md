# Phase 1: fix the core defects and prove it

- **Dates:** 3 October 2026
- **Plan:** tasks 1.1 to 1.9; findings F1 to F5, F21
- **Commits:** `d811521` (invariant tests), `cdc8e83` (F1 to F4, safe solve),
  `47d7b9d` (brakes), `4c59a64` (plan), tag `v0.2.0` on `main`
- **Written:** reconstructed on 5 October 2026 from the plan's results and
  the commit messages

## Goal

F1 to F5 fixed, and a permanent test suite that would have caught them.

## What was done

1. **Tests first (1.1).** The review's checks became Catch2 tests under
   `tests/invariants/`, for every joint type as a root and as the child of a
   rotating parent, at seeded random states: velocities against finite
   differences of the pose and against the Jacobian; inverse dynamics with and
   without acceleration differing by exactly the mass matrix times the
   acceleration; the velocity-dependent force against Lagrange's equations;
   energy and momentum conserved over a simulated second. For every
   constraint type: Jacobian and acceleration term against first and second
   finite differences of the constraint. Run on the unfixed engine they
   failed **315 of 845 assertions, exactly in the cases F1 to F4 predict and
   nowhere else.** That is the evidence the suite can see these defects.
2. **The fixes (1.2 to 1.5).** F1 to F3 with the review's patch, with the
   velocity and acceleration passes rewritten under clear names instead of
   left as patched; F4 by a new point-on-line constraint, two independent
   equations with an analytic acceleration term (a McPherson corner: 3
   independent equations for 4 tree coordinates).
3. **A safe constraint solve (1.6).** The unchecked factorisation became one
   that detects rank deficiency from its pivots and falls back to a
   rank-revealing solve; the multipliers are returned; a redundant set and a
   failed projection are reported once each.
4. **The brakes (1.7, F5).** Every wheel got a spin state that feeds its tyre;
   before, undriven tyres were forced to roll freely, so the front brakes of
   a rear-drive car produced no force, which was the cause of the two failing
   drivetrain tests. Wheel spin is advanced with the tyre's slip stiffness
   taken implicitly, stable at 1 ms down to standstill; the brake acts as dry
   friction and can hold a stationary wheel.
5. **Thresholds re-derived (1.8).** Each test whose threshold moved was
   rewritten with a value derived by hand in a comment: half-pedal braking
   measures 5.357 m/s² against 5.369 by hand.
6. **Gate (1.9).** Merged into `main`, tagged `v0.2.0`. 479 tests passed.

## What went wrong, and how it was found

- The drift check of the invariant tests could not at first tell RK4
  truncation from a real defect: it needed a 0.25 ms step to separate them.
- `BrakeParams::max_torque` meant three different things in three places, and
  the drivetrain applied twice the stated total. It now has one written
  definition: the total over the four wheels at full pedal.

## Verification

The invariant suite itself, which fails on the old code where it should and
passes on the new; the bead on the rotating rod at 1.22427 m (1e-8); the
universal joint's energy test at 1e-6 relative including rotational energy;
braking against the hand calculation.

## Lessons

- Write the test that fails first; a fix is only proven by a test that saw
  the defect.
- A tolerance must come from a calculation (working rule 3). The universal
  joint's 1 % tolerance had hidden an 80 % error in angular velocity.

## Open ends

About 0.2 % of the braking result comes from advancing wheel spin outside the
integrator; task 7.3 (wheels as bodies, D4) removes it.

## For the book

- "315 of 845, exactly where predicted": the strongest single argument for
  invariant testing, worth a figure (the failure pattern by joint type and
  parent motion).
- The implicit wheel-spin step: a small, complete example of treating a
  stiff term implicitly.
