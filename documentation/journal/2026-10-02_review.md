- **Dates:** 2 October 2026

- **Commits:** `859243e` (the snapshot reviewed), `37950f8` (plan and evidence)

## Goal

The author asked for the state of the project against its documentation, a
thorough review of the implementation and its documents, and a complete,
step-by-step plan to the finished program, including the CFD and real-time
work that the earlier plans left vague.

## Starting point

- 29 headers under `include/mbd/`, about 6,700 lines, header-only.
- 466 test cases; 464 passed. The two failures were in the drivetrain.
- Two earlier plans (`Development_schedule.txt`, `Multibody plan.pdf`),
  reference notes (`Knowledge.txt`, `Guides.txt`), a chapter selection of the
  textbooks, and five months of uncommitted work.
- The author's worry since June: that the solver errors meant the approach
  could not mature.

## What was done

1. Every header was read, with the tests around each suspected defect, the
   uncommitted changes, the two plans, `Knowledge.txt` and the Haug, Negrut,
   Serban and Solis paper on high-speed vehicle simulation. The textbooks
   were not read page by page; the plan relies on their standard content.
2. Each important suspicion was **tested, not argued**: a standalone program
   (`review_2026-10-02/verify.cpp`) compared the engine with finite
   differences of its own position-level quantities and with closed-form
   solutions. Its output before and after the proposed fixes, and a patch for
   three defects, were kept as evidence.
3. The findings were written up with identifiers F1 to F21, each marked
   *measured* or *read*.
4. Eight decisions were set out with a recommendation each (D1 to D8); three
   were the author's to take and were taken the same day: ISO 8855 axes
   (D3), OpenFOAM for CFD with an in-house solver flagged as a later goal
   (D6, task 11.12), and both real-time targets, Windows first (D7).
5. A 16-phase plan, each task with a "done when" condition, from making the
   builds safe to release, about 1,000 to 1,650 hours in all.

## What was found

The core had four defects of one family, all measured:

- **F1**, sliding joints on a rotating parent: a bead on a rod spinning at
  2 rad/s should travel from 0.500 m to 1.224 m in one second; the engine
  left it at 0.500 m.
- **F2**, the universal joint's motion subspace was written in the wrong
  frame: an angular-velocity error of 1.02 rad/s out of 1.28. Its energy test
  passed only because its tolerance was 1 % of a total dominated by potential
  energy.
- **F3**, the distance constraint lacked the centripetal terms of anchors away
  from the body origins (every tie rod and multilink link), hidden by the
  post-step projection.
- **F4**, the McPherson strut constraints had no acceleration term at all and
  three equations of rank two.

Their common cause: the same joint kinematics was written by hand in several
places, and the copies disagreed. The remedy proposed was structural (D1: one
kinematics kernel), not a fourth round of patches.

The vehicle layer had further defects (F5 to F12: brakes inactive on
undriven wheels, wheel spin outside the multibody model, a tyre without load
sensitivity, a mirrored vehicle frame, steering by editing constraints), the
program lacked whole capabilities (F13 to F18: implicit integration,
statics, reactions, contact, lap simulation beyond a point mass, interfaces),
and the project's hygiene needed work (F19 to F21: uncommitted work, builds
that could overload the machine, tests that asserted observed rather than
derived values).

The detailed sedan ran at 0.84 to 1.43 ms per 1 ms step (F15), at the edge of
real time on average and two to three times too slow in the worst step.

## Verification

Each measured finding came with a check the fix could be held to: the bead's
1.22427 m, the universal joint's error falling to 1e-11, the distance
constraint's to 3e-8. These later became the invariant tests of Phase 1.

## Lessons

- A finite-difference comparison found in an afternoon what months of
  scenario tests had not (finding F21): the scenario tests checked plausible
  behaviour, not the equations.
- The author's worry was answered with evidence: the formulation (joint
  coordinates on a tree, constraints to close loops) is the standard one for
  vehicle and real-time codes; the defects were implementation errors of one
  family.

## For the book

- The review method deserves a chapter: "How to find out whether a solver is
  right", with the bead on a spinning rod as the running example (closed form
  `r = r0 cosh(w t)`), finite differences of position-level quantities, and
  conservation laws.
- Figure: the four defects as four disagreeing copies of one formula.
- The structure of the master plan (findings, decisions, phases with "done
  when" conditions) is itself a method worth describing.
