# Multibody engine: review and master plan

Revision of 2 October 2026. This document replaces `Development_schedule.txt` (November 2025) and `Multibody plan.pdf` (February 2026) as the single roadmap. Both older plans are folded in here; nothing in them has been dropped.

It has four parts: where the project stands (section 1), what "finished" means (section 2), what the review found (section 3), and the plan itself (sections 4 to 7).

---

## 1. Where the project stands

**What was reviewed.** Every header under `include/mbd/` (29 files, about 6,700 lines), the test layout and the tests around each suspected defect, the uncommitted changes since the last commit, the two written plans, `Knowledge.txt` in full, and the Haug, Negrut, Serban and Solis paper on high-speed vehicle simulation. The textbooks (about 430 MB of PDFs) were not read page by page; where the plan cites them it relies on their standard content and on the chapter selection you made in `Selección 2`.

**How the findings were checked.** The important suspicions were tested with a standalone program that compares the engine against finite differences and against closed-form solutions. The program, its output, and a patch are in [review_2026-10-02/](review_2026-10-02/). Findings marked *measured* come from that program; findings marked *read* come from the code alone.

**The short version.**

- The engine builds, and 464 of 466 tests pass.
- The solver work from June and July holds. Chains of revolute and spherical joints, and a free joint attached to ground, agree with finite differences to nine digits, and energy is conserved.
- Four defects remain in the core, all of the same kind: a formula written by hand in several places that does not agree with itself. Three have a verified fix ready (section 3.1).
- The vehicle layer runs, but several of its models are placeholders: brakes act only on driven wheels, the default tyre has no load sensitivity, and the "equilibrium" start is not an equilibrium (section 3.2).
- Python, model files, results output, static analysis, stiff integration, real-time execution and CFD coupling do not exist yet.
- All work since 13 May 2026 is uncommitted.

**On your worry from June**, that the solver error might mean the project cannot mature. The review does not support that. The formulation you chose (joint coordinates on a tree, plus constraints to close loops) is the standard choice for vehicle and real-time codes, and it is the one `Knowledge.txt` recommends. The defects found are implementation errors of one family, not a flaw in the approach. The remedy is structural and is Phase 2 of this plan: compute each joint's kinematics in exactly one place, and test every joint and constraint against finite differences automatically.

## 2. What "finished" means

The plan aims at eight capabilities. Each has a test that says when it is done.

| # | Capability | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | General rigid multibody solver | Passes the IFToMM benchmark problems and agrees with an independent code to 1e-8 on random models |
| 2 | Vehicle model | Standard manoeuvres match a reference vehicle code and the linear bicycle model at small inputs |
| 3 | Vehicle builder | A complete car is defined in files, built in one call, and settles to a computed static equilibrium |
| 4 | Lap-time optimisation | Minimum lap time and racing line computed on a real track, then reproduced by the full car in closed loop |
| 5 | Aerodynamics from CFD | An aero map generated by automated CFD runs drives the vehicle model, with a converged ride-height loop |
| 6 | Real-time simulation | A 1 kHz model runs for ten minutes with a measured worst-case step under half the period |
| 7 | Python, optimisation and AI layer | Batch runs, optimisers, surrogates and a reinforcement-learning environment all drive the same engine |
| 8 | Flexible bodies and interoperability | Modal flexible body import, and export of the vehicle as an FMI unit |

### 3.1 Solver defects (wrong physics)

The "Where" links point to the code as reviewed. `model/system.hpp`, `model/joint.hpp`, `model/constraint.hpp` and `algorithms/dynamics.hpp` were removed in task 2.7b; the git history before that task holds them.

**F1. Sliding joints on a rotating parent are computed wrongly.** *Measured.*
When a prismatic or free joint sits on a body that rotates, the velocity and acceleration passes ignore the parent's rotation acting on the joint's own displacement. The mass matrix is right, so the two halves of the equations of motion disagree.
- Evidence: a bead sliding on a rod that spins at 2 rad/s should be flung outward from 0.500 m to 1.224 m in one second. The engine leaves it at 0.500 m. The velocity-dependent forces come out as [0.3, 0] against the exact [0.6, -2.0].
- On the simple vehicle (chassis plus prismatic wheels) the wheel velocity is off by 0.13 m/s and the velocity-dependent forces by about 5 % in the test case.
- Where: `compute_forward_velocities` in [system.hpp](../include/mbd/model/system.hpp), and the forward passes of `inverse_dynamics` and `compute_body_accelerations` in [dynamics.hpp](../include/mbd/algorithms/dynamics.hpp).
- Not affected: revolute and spherical joints, and a free joint whose parent is ground. That is why the double-wishbone path was unharmed.
- Fix verified: with the patch the bead reaches 1.22427 m, identical to the reference.

**F2. The universal joint reports the wrong angular velocity.** *Measured.*
Its motion subspace is written in the parent joint frame, while every algorithm treats it as written in the child frame.
- Evidence: angular velocity error of 1.02 rad/s out of 1.28 at joint angles (0.7, 0.5).
- The existing energy test passes only because its tolerance is 1 % of a total dominated by potential energy.
- Where: `UniversalCoordJoint` in [joint.hpp](../include/mbd/model/joint.hpp). No vehicle model uses this joint yet.
- Fix verified: error drops to 1e-11.

**F3. The distance constraint omits centripetal terms.** *Measured.*
When its attachment points are away from the body origins, the acceleration-level equation is short by about 5 % in the test case.
- This constraint is every tie rod and every link of the multilink suspension. The drift it causes is removed after each step by the projection, so it stays hidden, but the constraint forces during the step are wrong.
- Where: `DistanceConstraint::velocity_bias` in [constraint.hpp](../include/mbd/model/constraint.hpp).
- Fix verified: error drops to 3e-8.

**F4. The McPherson strut constraints are not valid for dynamics.** *Measured.*
Both strut-line constraints return zero for the velocity-dependent term (the comment says "sufficient for kinematic analysis"), so the acceleration-level equation is entirely wrong. They also use three equations where only two are independent, which makes the constraint matrix singular.
- Evidence: error of 2.07 against a true value of 2.06. Singular values of the constraint matrix for one corner: 4.26, 1.16, 0.039, 0.
- The dynamic McPherson model is held together by the post-step projection alone.
- Where: `StrutLineConstraint` and `StrutLineTwoBodyConstraint` in [constraint.hpp](../include/mbd/model/constraint.hpp). No fix written yet; task 1.5.

### 3.2 Vehicle model defects

**F5. Brakes do nothing on wheels that are not driven.** *Measured.*
The drivetrain forces every non-driven tyre to roll freely. On the rear-drive car the front wheels receive 1,950 Nm each and produce zero braking force. This is the failing braking test: the car decelerates at 3.7 m/s² where the test expects more than 7.
- Where: `apply_to_tires` in [drivetrain.hpp](../include/mbd/vehicle/drivetrain.hpp).
- The two failing tests and `drivetrain.hpp` are unchanged since the last commit apart from include paths, so the change in outcome comes from the July solver fixes. I did not rebuild the old commit to confirm they passed there.

**F6. Wheel spin lives outside the multibody model.** *Read.*
It is a separate state advanced by an explicit step in a callback. Consequences:
- No gyroscopic effect, and drive and brake torque reactions are not routed to the correct bodies. All longitudinal tyre force is applied at the contact patch of a non-rotating upright, which is right for outboard brakes and wrong for driven wheels (anti-squat will be wrong).
- Wheel inertia is hard-coded (0.5 × 40 kg × 0.35² m²), the front tyre radius is used at all four corners, and wheels cannot rotate backwards.
- `BrakeParams::max_torque` means three different things in three places. The drivetrain applies twice the stated total.

**F7. The default tyre has no load sensitivity.** *Measured.*
Peak friction goes from 1.0005 to 0.9990 between 2 kN and 8 kN, and cornering stiffness is a constant 45 kN/rad. Lateral load transfer therefore barely changes axle grip, so roll-stiffness distribution cannot shift the balance of the car. From reading the code: the tyre also has no aligning moment, camber thrust, rolling resistance or lag, and below 1 m/s the slip calculation is clamped, so a car held on its brakes on a slope will creep instead of standing still.

**F8. The tyre contact point is fixed in the upright.** *Read.*
It is the point (0, -R, 0) in the carrier body, so it swings forward when the body pitches. The road is a flat plane at a fixed height. Where: [tire.hpp](../include/mbd/forces/tire.hpp).

**F9. Mass, inertia and equilibrium of the double-wishbone and McPherson cars.** *Measured.*
- The wheel mass is dropped: the all-double-wishbone sedan has 1,500 kg in the model against 1,560 kg reported by the template. The sports car preset has 1,300 kg against 1,340 kg.
- Each control arm has its centre of mass at its chassis pivot, and arm and upright inertias are fixed boxes.
- Spring preload assumes 4,000 N per corner regardless of the car.
- At the state produced by `set_vehicle_equilibrium` the chassis accelerates at 1.08 m/s² and the largest coordinate acceleration is 73. There is no static solver.
- `steering_ratio`, `max_steer_angle` and `R_loaded_approx` are declared and never read, and `cg_offset` never reaches the multibody model.

**F10. Steering is not a rigid rack.** *Measured.*
The two tie-rod inner points move by different amounts (10.24 mm left, 9.65 mm right at 0.1 rad), and they are moved by editing the constraint between steps. The steering rate therefore never enters the velocity and acceleration equations.

**F11. The vehicle frame is mirrored.** *Read.*
The code uses X forward, Y up, and calls +Z "left". With X forward and Y up, +Z is geometrically the right-hand side. The code is consistent with itself, but it is a mirror image of ISO 8855, and "up is world Y" is written into the tyre, aerodynamics, anti-roll bar and equilibrium code. This will hurt when importing hardpoints, track data and CFD results.

**F12. The aerodynamic model cannot receive CFD results.** *Read.*
One centre of pressure, constant drag and downforce areas, a linear ride-height term. No lift, side force, yaw dependence, front and rear balance, or wind.

### 3.3 Capability gaps

**F13. Integration.** Fixed-step RK4 and semi-implicit Euler only. No error control and no implicit method, so stiff elements such as bushings cannot be simulated.

**F14. Analyses.** No static equilibrium, no assembly of initial conditions, no linearisation, no prescribed motions, no joint limits or friction, no contact other than the tyre. The Lagrange multipliers are computed and discarded, so there are no joint or constraint reaction forces to report.

**F15. Speed.** *Measured, on this laptop (i7-13700H), single thread.*

| Model | Average per 1 ms step | Worst step | Speed relative to real time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple sedan, 10 degrees of freedom, RK4 | 0.12 to 0.19 ms | 0.6 to 1.1 ms | 5 to 8 times faster |
| All-double-wishbone sedan, 26 degrees of freedom, RK4 | 0.84 to 1.43 ms | 2.2 to 2.9 ms | 0.7 to 1.2 times |

The ranges are the spread over four runs. The detailed car is at the edge of real time on average and misses the deadline by a factor of two to three in the worst step. Causes: memory allocation in every routine, a dense Jacobian per body for the mass matrix, the mass matrix rebuilt inside each projection iteration, and finite differences inside the free-joint velocity term.

**F16. Structure.**
- Two parallel vehicle interfaces (`VehicleModel`, `VehicleHandle`) with about 100 duplicated lines in the drivetrain.
- The legacy single-body code in `dynamics_og.hpp` is still included by the main path, and the legacy solver `solvers/solver.hpp` survives for one test file.
- Force elements cache results in `mutable` members, so they are not thread-safe and hold values from the last Runge-Kutta stage. A system cannot be copied, which blocks parallel batch runs.
- `RevoluteJoint` is a constraint and `RevoluteCoordJoint` is a joint.
- Size checks disappear in release builds, `<cassert>` is included inside the namespace ([core.hpp:116](../include/mbd/core/core.hpp#L116)), and there is a stray `};` at [drivetrain.hpp:432](../include/mbd/vehicle/drivetrain.hpp#L432).

**F17. Lap simulation.** Point mass only, with friction fixed at 1.0 regardless of the tyre. `Track::from_polyline` gives every segment the heading of the first, so only its curvature is usable. No track width. No link to the multibody car.

**F18. Interfaces.** The Python module exposes two demonstration functions. There is no model file format, no results output beyond one CSV writer, and no visualisation.

### 3.4 Project hygiene

**F19. Five months of work is uncommitted**, including the whole restructured tree. `documentation/` is untracked and holds 434 MB, almost all of it textbooks that should not be pushed to GitHub.

**F20. Builds.** The engine is header-only, so each of the 44 test files recompiles all of it. The rule that builds must use one job exists only in assistant memory, not in the build system. This laptop has 16 GB of memory and 20 logical cores, so a default parallel build can exhaust memory. CI builds in Debug with default parallelism, dependencies are fetched without hashes, there are no warning flags, and there is no README.

**F21. Tests.** There are 466 cases, with two weaknesses. Some tolerances are too loose to catch real errors (F2), and several tests assert behaviour that was observed, not behaviour that was derived (the two drivetrain failures). No test compares a joint or constraint against finite differences, which is the check that found F1 to F4 in an afternoon.

## 4. Decisions to take before coding

Each has a recommendation. Where the choice is yours, it says so.

**D1. One kinematics kernel.** Replace the three hand-written propagation loops with one implementation based on six-dimensional spatial vectors (Featherstone's formulation, as used by Pinocchio, Drake and Simbody). Every algorithm then reads the same per-joint quantities. This removes the cause of F1 to F4 and of the three bugs fixed in July.

**D2. Quaternions for free and spherical joints.** Store orientation as a unit quaternion and use the body-frame angular velocity as the velocity coordinate. The rotation-vector singularity, the canonicalisation step and the finite-difference velocity term all disappear, and the motion subspace becomes constant. The cost is that position and velocity vectors have different lengths, which the integrators must handle. Recommended, as part of D1.

**D3. ISO 8855 axes.** X forward, Y left, Z up, gravity along -Z. The core takes gravity and the road normal as inputs and assumes nothing. *Decided on 2 October 2026: yes.* The migration is task 7.1, and no new vehicle code is written in the old frame.

**D4. Wheels as real rotating bodies.** A revolute spin joint between upright and wheel. Spin becomes part of the multibody state, gyroscopic effects appear, and torque reactions go to the right bodies.

**D5. A compiled library.** Move non-template code into `.cpp` files. Tests then compile only themselves, and builds stop being a hazard.

**D6. CFD by coupling first.** Use OpenFOAM for the flow and connect it to the vehicle model. *Decided on 2 October 2026: OpenFOAM for Phase 11.* **Flagged: a CFD solver written from scratch is a stated goal of yours for later.** It is tracked as task 11.12, staged so that it never blocks the vehicle work, and validated against the OpenFOAM results that Phase 11 produces. It is a project of roughly the same size as the multibody solver.

**D7. Real-time target.** *Decided on 2 October 2026: both.* Soft real time on Windows first (tasks 12.5 to 12.8), then hard real time on Linux with the real-time kernel (task 12.10).

**D8. Files and tools.** JSON for model files, Python for scripting and post-processing, Rerun or Meshcat for three-dimensional playback, CasADi with IPOPT for optimal control.

### Phase 4. Time integration

Goal: accurate integration for ordinary models and stable integration for stiff ones.

- [ ] **4.1 Integrator interface.** One interface, with extra first-order states (tyre lag, actuators, controllers) registered by the elements that own them. This replaces the pre-step and post-step callbacks. *Done when the drivetrain no longer uses callbacks.*
- [ ] **4.2 Explicit methods.** Keep RK4 and semi-implicit Euler. Add an embedded pair (Dormand-Prince) with error control and dense output. *Done when measured convergence order matches theory.*
- [ ] **4.3 Implicit method for stiff models.** Start with generalised-alpha (HHT). Use finite-difference Jacobians first, then the analytic derivatives from 3.1. *Done when a spring 1,000 times too stiff for RK4 at 1 ms runs stably.*
- [ ] **4.4 Higher-order implicit.** The five-stage, fourth-order, L-stable SDIRK scheme described by Haug et al. *Done when it beats 4.3 on the bushed benchmark at tight tolerance.*
- [ ] **4.5 Fixed-cost implicit step.** Linearly implicit Euler with a single linear solve. This is the real-time integrator. *Done when its cost per step does not vary.*
- [ ] **4.6 Multirate.** Advance fast states (wheel spin, tyre lag) several times per main step, slowest first, as in Haug et al. section 6. *Done when a ratio of 6 reproduces the single-rate result.*
- [ ] **4.7 Constraints per integrator.** Projection for explicit methods, stabilised index-2 form for implicit methods, and a drift channel in the output. *Done when drift stays under tolerance for 1,000 simulated seconds.*
- [ ] **4.8 Sparse solve.** Sparse assembly and factorisation above a size threshold. *Done when dense and sparse results agree and the crossover size is measured.*
- [ ] **4.9 Verification.** Convergence plots, energy drift tables and work-against-precision curves for each method. *Done when stored in `docs/integrators.md`.*

## 7. Working rules

1. **Builds use the preset.** One compiler at a time until task 0.3 says otherwise.
2. **Every joint, constraint and force passes the invariant tests** before anything is built on it.
3. **Tests assert derived values.** A threshold comes with the hand calculation that justifies it. A test that only records what the code did is not a test.
4. **One place per formula.** If two routines need the same quantity, one computes it and the other reads it.
5. **Commit every session.** `main` stays green. Work happens on branches.
6. **Each phase ends at its gate.** The next phase starts when the "done when" conditions hold, not when the code exists.

## 8. Risks

- **The laptop.** A default parallel build can exhaust its 16 GB. Sustained all-core load also matters for CFD, which runs every core for hours. Before Phase 11, check that the machine survives a long all-core stress test, or plan to run CFD elsewhere.
- **Tyre data.** Real `.tir` files are proprietary. Use the published example sets and the open Formula SAE tyre test data until you have your own.
- **No measured vehicle data.** Validation therefore rests on analytic cases and on agreement with independent codes. If you can obtain test data for one real car, Phase 9 becomes much stronger.
- **Scope.** Phases 10 to 14 are each large. The milestones exist so that the project is useful at M2 even if later phases slip.
